Theorem 1: Every finite division ring is commutative (Wedderburn)

Statement: Every finite division ring is commutative (Wedderburn)

Hypotheses: R finite division ring

Conclusion: R is a field (commutative)

Definitions: division ring, field, finite

Proof sketch:

Step 1: For each a in R*, consider centralizer C(a)

Step 2: Class equation on R* under conjugation

Step 3: Each C(a) is a division subring

Step 4: By induction on dimension, C(a) is a field

Step 5: Show [R:C(a)] must equal 1 for all a

Step 6: Conclude R = C(a) for all a, hence commutative

Theorem 1: Wedderburn: every finite division ring is a field

Statement: Wedderburn: every finite division ring is a field

Hypotheses: R finite division ring

Conclusion: R is commutative (hence a field)

Definitions: division ring, commutativity, finite

Proof:

Step 1: Center $Z(R)$ is a finite field $\mathbb{F}_q$ by Wedderburn's little theorem.

Step 2: Class equation for $R^*$: $|R^*| = |Z(R^*)| + \sum [R^* : C_{R^*}(g_i)]$.

Step 3: Each $[R^*:C_{R^*}(g_i)]$ is a power of $q > 1$, but $|R^*|$ is also a power of $q$.

Step 4: Deduce all elements commute with central elements, hence $R$ is commutative.

Theorem 2: CRT for rings: $R/

Statement: CRT for rings: $R/

Hypotheses: coprime ideals $I+J=R$

Conclusion: quotient is product

Definitions: Chinese Remainder, coprime ideals

Proof:

Step 1: Define $\phi: R \to R/I \times R/J$ by $\phi(r) = (r+I, r+J)$.

Step 2: Surjective: $1 = i + j$ with $i \in I, j \in J$, so $\phi(j) = (j+I, 1+J)$.

Step 3: Kernel $= I \cap J$. First isomorphism theorem gives result.
