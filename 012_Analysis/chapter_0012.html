Theorem 1: MVT: f continuous on [a,b], diff on (a,b) => f'(c) = (f(b)-f(a))/(b-a)

Statement: MVT: f continuous on [a,b], diff on (a,b) => f'(c) = (f(b)-f(a))/(b-a)

Hypotheses: f continuous on [a,b], f differentiable on (a,b)

Conclusion: exists c in (a,b) with f'(c) = avg slope

Definitions: continuous, differentiable, mean value

Proof sketch:

Step 1: Define g(x) = f(x) - f(a) - [(f(b)-f(a))/(b-a)]*(x-a)

Step 2: g(a) = g(b) = 0

Step 3: g is continuous on [a,b] and differentiable on (a,b)

Step 4: By Rolle's theorem, g'(c) = 0 for some c in (a,b)

Step 5: g'(c) = f'(c) - (f(b)-f(a))/(b-a) = 0

Step 6: Therefore f'(c) = (f(b)-f(a))/(b-a)

Theorem 2: Heine-Borel: closed bounded subset of R^n is compact

Statement: Heine-Borel: closed bounded subset of R^n is compact

Hypotheses: K subset R^n, K closed, K bounded

Conclusion: K is compact

Definitions: closed, bounded, compact, Heine-Borel

Proof sketch:

Step 1: K bounded => K subset [-M,M]^n (closed cube)

Step 2: Cover K by open sets in R^n

Step 3: Extend cover to cover the cube

Step 4: Cube is compact (standard result for products of intervals)

Step 5: Closed subset of compact space is compact

Theorem 3: DCT: f_n -> f a.e., |f_n| integral f_n -> integral f

Statement: DCT: f_n -> f a.e., |f_n| integral f_n -> integral f

Hypotheses: f_n measurable, f_n -> f a.e., |f_n| <= g, g integrable

Conclusion: integral f_n converges to integral f

Definitions: dominated convergence, integral, Measurability

Proof sketch:

Step 1: Apply Fatou's lemma to g + f_n >= 0

Step 2: liminf integral(g + f_n) >= integral(g + f)

Step 3: integral(g) + liminf integral(f_n) >= integral(g) + integral(f)

Step 4: Apply Fatou's lemma to g - f_n >= 0

Step 5: liminf integral(g - f_n) >= integral(g - f)

Step 6: integral(g) + liminf integral(-f_n) >= integral(g) - integral(f)

Step 7: Combine: lim integral(f_n) = integral(f)

Theorem 4: Bolzano-Weierstrass: bounded sequence in R has convergent subsequence

Statement: Bolzano-Weierstrass: bounded sequence in R has convergent subsequence

Hypotheses: sequence (x_n) bounded in R

Conclusion: convergent subsequence exists

Definitions: bounded, convergent, subsequence, nested intervals

Proof sketch:

Step 1: Sequence lies in some [a,b]

Step 2: Bisect [a,b], one half contains infinitely many terms

Step 3: Choose that half as [a_1,b_1]

Step 4: Repeat: find nested intervals [a_n,b_n] with diam -> 0

Step 5: Each contains infinitely many terms

Step 6: Pick x_{n_k} from k-th interval

Step 7: (x_{n_k}) is Cauchy hence convergent
