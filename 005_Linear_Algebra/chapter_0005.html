Theorem 1: Every linear transformation T: V -> V has eigenvalue (over alg. closed field)

Statement: Every linear transformation T: V -> V has eigenvalue (over alg. closed field)

Hypotheses: V finite-dimensional, F algebraically closed, T: V -> V linear

Conclusion: T has at least one eigenvalue

Definitions: eigenvalue, characteristic polynomial, algebraically closed

Proof sketch:

Step 1: Choose basis, represent T as matrix A

Step 2: Compute characteristic polynomial p(t) = det(A - t I)

Step 3: p(t) has degree n = dim(V) > 0

Step 4: F algebraically closed => p has root lambda

Step 5: A v = lambda v for nonzero v, so lambda is eigenvalue

Theorem 2: Spectral theorem: self-adjoint operator has orthonormal eigenbasis

Statement: Spectral theorem: self-adjoint operator has orthonormal eigenbasis

Hypotheses: Let G be a group with eigenbasis, linear algebra, operator, orthonormal, self-adjoint

Conclusion: The structure of G satisfies the given properties

Definitions: groups, homomorphisms, eigenbasis, linear algebra, operator, orthonormal, self-adjoint

Proof sketch:

Step 1: Show all eigenvalues of real symmetric matrices are real: if Av = lambda*v, then lambda = / = / = conj(lambda).

Step 2: Eigenspaces for distinct eigenvalues are orthogonal: if Av=lv, Aw=mw, l!=m, then l = = = m, so =0.

Step 3: By induction on dimension: pick eigenvector v_1, restrict A to {v_1}^perp, apply inductive hypothesis.

Step 4: Collect orthonormal eigenvectors as columns of Q. Then Q^T*A*Q = D (diagonal).

Step 5: Therefore A = Q*D*Q^T is the spectral decomposition.
