Theorem 1: Lagrange: $|H|$ divides $|G|$ for subgroup $H$ of finite $G$

Statement: Lagrange: $|H|$ divides $|G|$ for subgroup $H$ of finite $G$

Hypotheses: G finite, $H \leq G$

Conclusion: $|H|$ divides $|G|$

Definitions: left coset, partition, index

Proof:

Step 1: Define left cosets $aH = \{ah: h \in H\}$. Two cosets are equal or disjoint.

Step 2: Cosets partition $G$ into equivalence classes, each with exactly $|H|$ elements.

Step 3: Total: $|G| = [G:H] \cdot |H|$, so $|H|$ divides $|G|$.

Theorem 2: Cauchy: prime $p \mid |G|$ implies element of order $p$ exists

Statement: Cauchy: prime $p \mid |G|$ implies element of order $p$ exists

Hypotheses: G finite, $p$ prime, $p \mid |G|$

Conclusion: exists $g \in G$ with $\text{ord}(g)=p$

Definitions: orbit-stabilizer, fixed point

Proof:

Step 1: Let $S = \{(g_1,...,g_p): g_1\cdots g_p=e\}$. $C_p$ acts by cyclic permutation.

Step 2: Orbits have size 1 or $p$. $|S|=|G|^{p-1}$, divisible by $p$.

Step 3: Fixed points: $(g,...,g)$ with $g^p=e$. Count $\equiv 0 \pmod{p}$.

Step 4: At least $p-1$ non-identity fixed points, each giving element of order $p$.

Theorem 3: Sylow theorems: existence, conjugacy, and counting of Sylow $p$-subgroups

Statement: Sylow theorems: existence, conjugacy, and counting of Sylow $p$-subgroups

Hypotheses: G finite, $p^k \parallel |G|$

Conclusion: Sylow $p$-subgroups exist, are conjugate, $n_p \equiv 1 \pmod{p}$

Definitions: Sylow, conjugacy, orbit-stabilizer

Proof:

Step 1: Existence by induction on $|G|$ using class equation and Cauchy's theorem.

Step 2: Conjugacy: $G$ acts on Sylow subgroups by conjugation; orbits are conjugacy classes.

Step 3: Counting: $n_p = [G:N_G(P)]$ divides $m$ (where $|G|=p^km$), and $n_p \equiv 1 \pmod{p}$.

Theorem 4: First isomorphism theorem: $G/\ker

Statement: First isomorphism theorem: $G/\ker

Hypotheses: homomorphism $\phi: G \to H$

Conclusion: $G/\ker(\phi) \cong \text{im}(\phi)$

Definitions: homomorphism, kernel, isomorphism

Proof:

Step 1: Define $\psi: G/\ker(\phi) \to \text{im}(\phi)$ by $\psi(gK) = \phi(g)$.

Step 2: Well-defined: $gK = hK \Rightarrow h^{-1}g \in \ker \Rightarrow \phi(g) = \phi(h)$.

Step 3: Injective: $\psi(gK)=e \Rightarrow g \in K \Rightarrow gK = K$.

Step 4: Surjective by definition of image.

Statement: Burnside's lemma: $|X/G| = \frac{1}{|G|}\sum |\text{Fix}

Conclusion: number of orbits = average fixed points

Statement: Cayley: every group embeds in $\text{Sym}
