Theorem 1: RSA security based on difficulty of factoring large integers

Statement: RSA security based on difficulty of factoring large integers

Hypotheses: n = pq large primes, e coprime to phi(n)

Conclusion: easy to encrypt (mod n), hard to decrypt without factorization

Definitions: RSA, factoring, trapdoor

Proof sketch:

Step 1: Public key: (n,e)Encrypt: c = m^e mod n

Step 2: Private key: d = e^{-1} mod phi(n)

Step 3: Decrypt: m = c^d mod n

Step 4: Breaking RSA requires knowing phi(n) = (p-1)(q-1)

Step 5: Finding phi(n) requires factoring n

Theorem 3: Uniqueness theorem: canonical objects are unique up to natural equivalence

Statement: Uniqueness theorem: canonical objects are unique up to natural equivalence

Hypotheses: Given assumptions in cryptography

Conclusion: The stated property holds

Definitions: fundamental definitions in cryptography

Proof sketch:

Step 1: Since ed = 1 mod phi(n), we have ed = 1 + k*phi(n) for some k.

Step 2: If gcd(m,p) = 1: m^{p-1} = 1 mod p by Fermat, so m^{ed} = m^{1+k(p-1)(q-1)} = m mod p.

Step 3: If m mod p = 0: m^{ed} = 0 = m mod p trivially.

Step 4: Similarly m^{ed} = m mod q by CRT (or same argument).

Step 5: By CRT: m^{ed} = m mod pq = m mod n.
