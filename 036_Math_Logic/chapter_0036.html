Theorem 1: Gödel First Incompleteness: consistent r.e. theory expressing arithmetic is incomplete

Statement: Gödel First Incompleteness: consistent r.e. theory expressing arithmetic is incomplete

Hypotheses: S consistent, S recursively enumerable, S expresses basic arithmetic

Conclusion: S is incomplete (there exists G neither provable nor disprovable in S)

Definitions: Gödel, incomplete, consistent, r.e.

Proof sketch:

Step 1: Arithmetize syntax: encode formulas and proofs as natural numbers

Step 2: Construct Gödel sentence G: 'G is not provable in S'

Step 3: If S proves G, then G is true (S sound), but G says 'not provable'

Step 4: Contradiction: S proves something false about itself

Step 5: If S disproves G, then G is false, so G IS provable in S

Step 6: But then S proves both G and not-G, contradicting consistency

Step 7: Therefore G is independent of S

Theorem 3: Halting problem is undecidable

Statement: Halting problem is undecidable

Hypotheses: Turing machine M, input w

Conclusion: no algorithm decides if M halts on w

Definitions: halting problem, Turing, undecidable

Proof sketch:

Step 1: Assume decider H exists

Step 2: Construct machine D that runs H on (M,M) then does opposite

Step 3: D(D) halts iff H(D,D) says doesn't halt

Step 4: Contradiction: D(D) halts <=> D(D) doesn't halt

Theorem 8: Complete theory: every sentence or its negation is provable

Statement: Complete theory: every sentence or its negation is provable

Conclusion: for every sentence phi, T proves phi or T proves not phi

Definitions: complete theory, decidable, consistent

Proof sketch:

Step 1: If T doesn't prove phi, then T + not phi is consistent

Step 2: Complete: exactly one of phi, not phi is provable

Step 3: Decidable: can determine which

Step 4: Peano arithmetic is incomplete (Gödel)

Step 5: Theory of algebraically closed fields is complete
