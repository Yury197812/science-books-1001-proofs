Theorem 1: Gauss-Bonnet: integral K dA = 2pi*chi(M)

Statement: Gauss-Bonnet: integral K dA = 2pi*chi(M)

Hypotheses: M compact oriented surface

Conclusion: integral K dA = 2pi*chi(M)

Definitions: Gauss-Bonnet, curvature, Euler char

Proof sketch:

Step 1: For compact oriented surface M without boundary.

Step 2: integral_M K dA = 2*pi*chi(M) where K is Gaussian curvature, chi = V-E+F.

Step 3: Proof: triangulate M, apply local Gauss-Bonnet to each triangle.

Step 4: Sum of angle defects equals total curvature.

Step 5: Connect to Euler characteristic via Gauss-Bonnet for polyhedra.

Theorem 5: Stewart's theorem: b^2*m + c^2*n = a(d^2 + mn) for cevian d

Statement: Stewart's theorem: b^2*m + c^2*n = a(d^2 + mn) for cevian d

Hypotheses: triangle ABC, D on BC, BD=m, DC=n, AD=d, AB=c, AC=b

Definitions: Stewart, cevian, side lengths

Theorem 25: Pythagorean theorem: a^2 + b^2 = c^2 for right triangles

Statement: Pythagorean theorem: a^2 + b^2 = c^2 for right triangles

Hypotheses: right triangle with legs a,b

Conclusion: a^2 + b^2 = c^2

Definitions: Pythagorean, right triangle, hypotenuse

Proof sketch:

Step 1: Given right triangle with legs a, b and hypotenuse c.

Step 2: Construct squares on each side. Area of large square = c^2.

Step 3: Rearrange four copies of the triangle inside the square.

Step 4: Remaining area = a^2 + b^2 (two smaller squares).

Step 5: Therefore c^2 = a^2 + b^2.
