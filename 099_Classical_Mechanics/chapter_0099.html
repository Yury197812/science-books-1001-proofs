EMPIRICAL_LAW: Newton's Second Law: F = ma

Statement: Newton's Second Law: F = ma

Hypotheses: classical regime, inertial frame

Conclusion: F = ma

Definitions: force, mass, acceleration

Proof sketch:

Step 1: F = ma relates force (F), mass (m), and acceleration (a) for point particles.

Step 2: Newton's first law (inertia): F=0 => a=0 (object at rest stays at rest).

Step 3: Newton's third law: for every action F, there's equal and opposite reaction -F.

Step 4: For systems: F_net = m*a_cm where a_cm is center of mass acceleration.

Step 5: Limitations: non-relativistic (v << c), non-quantum (S >> h-bar).

Theorem 2: Hamilton's equations: $\dot{q} = \frac{\partial H}{\partial p}$, $\dot{p} = -\frac{\partial H}{\partial q}$

Statement: Hamilton's equations: $\dot{q} = \frac{\partial H}{\partial p}$, $\dot{p} = -\frac{\partial H}{\partial q}$

Hypotheses: Hamiltonian $H(q,p,t)$

Conclusion: canonical equations of motion

Definitions: Hamilton, canonical, phase space

Proof:

Step 1: Legendre transform: $H(q,p,t) = p\dot{q} - L(q,\dot{q},t)$ where $p = \partial L/\partial \dot{q}$.

Step 2: Compute $dH = \dot{q} dp + p d\dot{q} - dL$.

Step 3: Substitute Euler-Lagrange $dL = \frac{\partial L}{\partial q}dq + \frac{d}{dt}\frac{\partial L}{\partial \dot{q}}d\dot{q}$.

Step 4: Rearrange: $\dot{q} = \partial H/\partial p$, $\dot{p} = -\partial H/\partial q$.
